## 1. The competitive set

**Tiering (Module 2):** the **primary** competitor is the status quo, which every organizer is already using right now. **Secondary** competitors solve part of the job. **Aspirational** competitors could grow into it.

| Competitor | What they do well | Where they're weak | How we win |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PRIMARY — The DIY stack** (group chat + Venmo/Splitwise + spreadsheet) | Free, familiar, no sign-up, flexible for any destination. Venmo launched Groups to track and settle shared expenses *[S6]*, and Splitwise is free (Pro $4.99/month) *[S4]*. | Nothing in it books anything. Splitwise is "not a trip planner" *[S4]*, so the organizer still fronts the booking, tracks replies by hand and chases money afterward. ## 2. Battlecard

**Primary opponent: the DIY stack.** The Module 2 lab asks for a named competitor, so Viator / GetYourGuide is covered in the quick card below. But more than half of sales processes end in "no decision" (Module 2), and the status quo is what Roamly's support team is watching organizers use today.

### Know the landscape

- **Overview:** Group chat for decisions, a payments app (Venmo, Splitwise, Zelle) for money, a spreadsheet or shared doc for the plan. Costs nothing; works for small, simple groups.
- **Key offerings:** Messaging, expense tracking and settle-up, a manual itinerary. No booking, no deadlines, no automatic reminders.
- **Pricing:** Free for basic use (Splitwise Pro $4.99/month) *[S4]*. Business payment tools charge about 1.9%–3.5% in processing fees *[S5]*.

### Understand the battlefield

- **Strengths:** zero cost; everyone already has it; no commitment; trusted payment brands; works for any trip.
- **Weaknesses:** the organizer still books and fronts the money; payments are chased by text; nothing connects who said yes to who paid to what's booked; it degrades exactly when stakes rise (20+ travelers, multi-thousand-dollar trips) *[S5]*. 45% of group travelers report financial conflict or discomfort *[S1]*.

### Win the deal

- **Why we win:** The organizer stops fronting and chasing. Each person pays their own share through one link, and the booking and plan live in the same place.
- **Why they win (be uncomfortable):** For a small group of close friends who already trust each other, "I'll just Venmo you" costs nothing and takes ten seconds. Invitees have to trust a platform they may not know. Organizers can plan *any* trip in the DIY stack, but Roamly only covers experiences on Roamly. If our invitee flow adds even a little friction, organizers will quietly go back to the chat.
- **Why us (vs. nothing):** Because the pain is the organizer's burden, not the booking. Roamly makes the organizer's job disappear, and the experiences are already vetted.

### Handle the hard moments

- **Quick dismiss:** "Venmo is great for settling up after. It doesn't book the tour, and it doesn't stop you being the one who fronts the money."
- **One-line advantage:** *Everyone pays their own share through one link, so the organizer never fronts the cost.*
- **Trap to set / question to plant:**
  - "Last group trip, how many people paid you late, and who covered it?"
  - "How many different apps did you need to get eight people on one plan?"
  - "If two friends dropped out a week before, who ate the cost?"
- **Objection → response:**
  - *"Venmo and Splitwise already work."* → They track money after the fact. Roamly collects each share before the booking is confirmed, so you don't front anything.
  - *"My friends won't sign up for another app."* → Invitees join from the link, see the plan and their exact share, and pay (account creation can come after payment, pending A3). This is the claim we will test first in the beta.
  - *"Viator is cheaper and has more options."* → Viator's catalog is bigger, and quality varies *[S2]*. Roamly's experiences are vetted, repeat booking runs at 3x the industry average *[Brief]*, and Roamly Groups is built for the organizer's job.
  - *"What if someone doesn't pay?"* → Each group has a payment deadline and a set rule for unpaid shares (to be finalized, A5). The organizer isn't left holding the bill by default.

### Quick card: Viator / GetYourGuide (named competitor)

- **Where they beat us:** catalog size, free cancellation on most products, brand reach, and price deals *[S2]*.
- **Where they're weakest for this ICP:** group-specific flows (no described split payment, and private groups are a niche) *[S2]*.
- **Landmine:** "How would you get eight people to pay for one booking on a tour site, and who has to front the total?"

## 3. The strategic bet

**Data → Insight → Belief → Bet**

- **Data (top three signals, ranked):**
  1. Roamly users are already forcing group bookings through the product, and drop-off is high *[Brief]*. This is behavior, not opinion.
  2. The market pain is financial and social: 45% of group travelers have experienced financial conflict or discomfort, and 82% would overpay to avoid money arguments *[S1]*.
  3. Every alternative solves one part: tour sites sell fixed group tours *[S2]*, Airbnb offers private groups without described payment splitting *[S3]*, and Wanderlog, Splitwise and SquadTrip each handle a single slice *[S4][S5]*.
- **Insight:** The job isn't "book for many people". It's "stop one person from absorbing the effort, the cost and the awkwardness." That is why the DIY stack survives (it's free and familiar) and why every point tool feels incomplete. The invitees, who each need an account and a payment, are also the growth engine.
- **Belief:** *I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when the organizer never has to front the money or chase anyone, because the cost of organizing, not the cost of the trip, is what makes people give up or fall back to the group chat.*

> **We are betting that** Roamly Groups wins by making the organizer's job disappear (one link gets the whole party in and paid, with no fronting and no chasing), **because** the organizer's burden is the unsolved problem that every alternative leaves in place, and the invitees they bring in create a product-led growth loop.

- **Why now:** The demand is visible in behavior *[Brief]*. Money friction on group trips is widespread and still unsolved by the tools people use *[S1][S4]*. Roamly's reputation and 12,000+ hosts across 40 cities *[Brief]* are an asset a point solution can't copy overnight.
- **What we're deliberately NOT doing (the sacrifice):**
  - No corporate or enterprise features at launch (invoicing, approvals, admin controls). The sales-led motion is deferred.
  - No paid acquisition at launch. The invite loop has to prove itself first.
  - No change to how individual bookings work.
  - Not trying to plan the whole trip (flights, lodging). We win on the experiences.
- **What would prove the bet right within 90 days** *(proposed thresholds, to be re-set against the week-0 baseline, A2)*:
  - At least 70% of invited participants pay within 72 hours.
  - At least two new-to-Roamly accounts per group, on average.
  - Group-session completion closes at least half the gap to individual-session completion.
  - Individual-booking conversion stays within ±2% of the holdout.

## 4. Evidence

- Roamly users are working around the product to book groups, and drop-off is high; feedback is consistent *[Brief]*.
- 54% of U.S. adults took a group trip in the past five years; 45% experienced financial conflict or discomfort; 82% would pay more than their fair share to avoid money arguments *[S1]*.
- Tour marketplaces sell fixed-departure group tours and don't specialize in private groups *[S2]*. Airbnb's private-group option depends on host opt-in and shows no payment splitting *[S3]*.
- Planning and payment tools are single-purpose: Splitwise doesn't plan, Wanderlog doesn't collect payment, SquadTrip has no shared itinerary editing, TripIt has no group features *[S4][S5]*.
- A group-trip design case study describes the experience as "stressful", "tedious" and "high-effort", with organizers juggling several tools *[S7]* (qualitative; single case study, not a quantified study).
- Roamly's own repeat booking runs at 3x the industry average, across 12,000+ hosts and 40 cities *[Brief]*.

**Caveat:** The competitor claims come from published reviews and help pages, not hands-on testing (A9). Before the bet is final, someone on the team should try the group flow on Viator, GetYourGuide and Airbnb directly.
