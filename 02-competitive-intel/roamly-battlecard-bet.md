# Roamly Groups · Competitive Battlecard & Strategic Bet, Module 2

## Primary Competitor
- **Name:** The DIY stack (status quo): a group chat + Venmo / Splitwise + a shared spreadsheet or doc. (Named secondary competitor: Viator / GetYourGuide, covered in a quick card inside the sections below.)
- **Core offering:** Messaging for decisions, a payments or expense app for money (Venmo Groups tracks and settles shared expenses; Splitwise splits costs), and a manual spreadsheet for the plan. Nothing in the stack books anything.
- **Positioning:** No formal positioning: it is the default. 'Free and familiar': no sign-up, works for any trip, and trusted payment brands. Venmo launched Groups in 2023 to let people track, split and settle shared expenses.
- **Primary audience:** Every group-trip organizer, especially Gen Z and Millennials, among whom group money friction is highest. Works best for small groups of close friends who already trust each other.
- **Pricing / packaging:** Free for basic use (Splitwise Pro $4.99/month). Business payment tools charge roughly 1.9%-3.5% in processing fees (e.g. Venmo business 1.9% + $0.10; PayPal invoicing 3.49% + $0.49).
- **Why they're the biggest threat:** It is already what Roamly's organizers use, it costs nothing, and more than half of sales processes end in 'no decision'. If Roamly's invitee flow adds friction, organizers quietly go back to the chat. It is also the workaround Support sees users falling back on today.

## Battlecard
### 1 · Know the Landscape
- **Competitor overview:** A group chat for decisions, a payments or expense app (Venmo, Splitwise, Zelle) for money, and a spreadsheet or shared doc for the plan. Costs nothing and works for small, simple groups. Secondary named competitor: Viator / GetYourGuide sell fixed-departure group tours with huge catalogs, and neither is described as specializing in private groups or split payment.
- **Key offerings:** Messaging, expense tracking and settle-up, a manual itinerary. No booking, no payment deadlines, no automatic reminders.
- **Pricing:** Free for basic use; Splitwise Pro $4.99/month; business payment fees about 1.9%-3.5%. (Viator keeps roughly 20% of gross bookings; GetYourGuide takes a supplier-specific commission.)

### 2 · Understand the Battlefield
- **Key strengths:** Zero cost, everyone already has it, no commitment, trusted payment brands, and it works for any trip anywhere.
- **Key weaknesses:** The organizer still books and fronts the money; payments are chased by text; nothing connects who said yes to who paid to what is booked; it degrades exactly when stakes rise (20+ travelers, multi-thousand-dollar trips). 45% of group travelers report financial conflict or discomfort (CIT / Harris Poll 2026).

### 3 · Win the Deal
- **Why you win:** The organizer stops fronting and chasing: each person pays their own share through one link, and the booking and the plan live in the same place.
- **Why they win:** For a small group of close friends who already trust each other, 'I'll just Venmo you' costs nothing and takes ten seconds. Invitees have to trust a platform they may not know. The DIY stack plans any trip, while Roamly only covers experiences on Roamly. If our invitee flow adds even a little friction, organizers go back to the chat. (Vs. Viator / GetYourGuide: they beat us on catalog size, free cancellation on most products and price deals.)
- **Why you?:** Because the pain is the organizer's burden, not the booking. Roamly makes the organizer's job disappear, and the experiences are already vetted by local hosts (repeat booking at 3x the industry average).

### 4 · Handle the Hard Moments
- **Quick dismiss:** 'Venmo is great for settling up after. It doesn't book the tour, and it doesn't stop you being the one who fronts the money.'
- **Landmine questions:** 'Last group trip, how many people paid you late, and who covered it?' / 'How many different apps did you need to get eight people on one plan?' / 'If two friends dropped out a week before, who ate the cost?' / (For Viator / GetYourGuide) 'How would you get eight people to pay for one booking on a tour site, and who has to front the total?'
- **Objection handling:** 'Venmo and Splitwise already work.' -> They track money after the fact; Roamly collects each share before the booking is confirmed, so you don't front anything. / 'My friends won't sign up for another app.' -> Invitees join from the link, see the plan and their exact share, and pay (account creation can come after payment, pending V1 scope); this is the first thing we test in beta. / 'Viator is cheaper and has more options.' -> Their catalog is bigger and quality varies; Roamly's hosts are vetted, repeat booking runs at 3x the industry average, and Groups is built for the organizer's job. / 'What if someone doesn't pay?' -> Each group has a payment deadline and a defined rule for unpaid shares (to be finalized before beta).

## Strategic Bet (Data → Insight → Belief → Bet)
### Data
- **Who Roamly Groups is for:** The friend who ends up organizing a 5-12 person group trip and is already a Roamly user: the organizer who fronts the money and chases replies.
- **The one reason to choose it:** The organizer never has to front the money or chase anyone: each person pays their own share through one link, in one place with the booking and the plan.
- **Biggest threat to that position:** The DIY stack: free, familiar and 'good enough' for small friend groups. Any extra friction for invitees sends organizers back to the group chat. Longer term, Airbnb could add split payment to its private-group Experiences.

### Insight
- **The signal that changes the market view:** The job is not 'book for many people'. It is 'stop one person absorbing the effort, the cost and the awkwardness'. That is why the DIY stack survives (free and familiar) and why every point tool feels incomplete: tour sites sell fixed group tours, Airbnb private groups show no payment splitting, and Wanderlog, Splitwise and SquadTrip each handle one slice. The invitees each organizer brings in are also the growth engine.

### Belief
- I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when the organizer never has to front the money or chase anyone, because the cost of organizing, not the cost of the trip, is what makes people give up or fall back to the group chat.

### Bet
- **I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by:** Making the organizer's job disappear (one link gets the whole party in and paid, with no fronting and no chasing), because that burden is the unsolved problem every alternative leaves in place, and the invitees organizers bring in create a product-led growth loop. 90-day proof (proposed): 70%+ of invitees pay within 72 hours; 2+ new accounts per group; individual-booking conversion within +/-2%.
- **To make this bet, I am deprioritizing:** Corporate / enterprise features at launch (invoicing, approvals, admin controls) and the sales-led motion; paid acquisition at launch; any change to how individual bookings work; and trying to plan whole trips (flights, lodging).
