# Roamly Groups · GTM Strategy, Module 1

## Discover Framework
### 1 · Align on the Goal
- **Business problem:** Roamly's core experience is loved (best-in-category ratings, repeat booking at 3x the industry average, 12,000+ vetted hosts in 40 cities) but users are forcing group bookings through a product built for individuals and small parties: separate accounts, manual split payments, hand-synced availability and high drop-off. Roamly is leaking high-intent demand to workarounds. Roamly Groups captures that demand without disrupting what already works.
- **Success looks like:** Beyond revenue. Product: group sessions complete instead of dropping off (north-star: fully paid group bookings per month). Brand: Roamly becomes the easy way to book a trip for a whole group. Customer relationship: organizers become repeat users and advocates, and invitees become new Roamly accounts. Guardrail: individual-booking conversion stays within +/-2% of a holdout.

### 2 · Do the Research
- **Signals of opportunity:** Behavioral: Support reports users coordinating group bookings across separate accounts, splitting payments by hand and syncing availability; drop-off on these sessions is high; feedback is consistent that people want to do this on Roamly. Retention is strong (3x industry repeat booking), so value is proven. Market: 54% of U.S. adults took a group trip in the last five years and 45% of group travelers report financial conflict or discomfort (CIT Bank / Harris Poll, May 2026, n = 2,067). Gaps: tour marketplaces sell fixed-departure group tours, and planning and payment apps each solve one slice. Not yet sized: next step is group-sized sessions x average basket from Roamly data, plus external travel research (assumption A2).
- **Candidate segments:** (1) Friend-group and celebration organizers, 5-12 people (chosen). (2) Corporate team offsites. (3) Family reunions and multi-generational trips. (4) Destination wedding parties. (5) Clubs, communities and student groups.

### 3 · Build a Working Team
- **Stakeholders:** Product and Engineering (V1 scope, invitee flow); Payments / Finance (margin, fraud, refunds, processing cost); Analytics (week-0 baseline and instrumentation); Customer Support and Customer Success (the group-session signal, first-wave readiness); Host Partnerships (capacity, deposits, party size); Product Marketing (positioning and messaging); Legal / Trust & Safety (payment terms, unpaid-share policy).

### 4 · Form Your Hypothesis
- **Segment to target first:** Friend-group and celebration organizers: the person who plans a 5-12 person trip (friends' trips, birthdays, bachelor/bachelorette weekends) and is already a Roamly user. Why first: the demand is already visible in Roamly's data, the product is ready for them (split payments, shared itinerary, group booking), they fit a product-led motion, and each organizer brings in invitees who become new accounts. Corporate offsites are deferred: higher contract value, but sales-led, and the product likely lacks invoicing and admin controls.
- **Core value proposition:** Plan the trip, not the paperwork: one link gets the whole party in and paid, each person pays their own share, and the organizer never fronts the money or chases anyone.

## GTM Motion
- **Primary motion:** PLG
- **Justification:** Four of the five inputs point to PLG: time to value is minutes (start a group, share a link, first payment lands); the organizer is both buyer and user; contract value is low to mid (tens to hundreds of dollars per booking); the sales cycle is self-serve; and it scales through product virality (each group invites roughly 4-11 people). A light community-led layer comes from organizer word of mouth. Sales-led is deliberately deferred until corporate / large-group demand is proven (tracked through a Concierge waitlist). Launch tier 2: a focused plan with a closed beta first.

## Strategic Inputs
- **Primary goal:** Expand
- **Target audience:** Primary: existing Roamly users who start or abandon a group-sized booking (5-12 people) and personally front the money and chase replies; skewing Gen Z and Millennial, where group money friction is highest (72% and 54% report money-related issues, CIT / Harris Poll 2026). Secondary (Acquire): the invitees they bring in, who are new to Roamly.

## Pressure-test
- **Assumption I'm challenging:** That invitees will join from a link and pay quickly with little friction. The whole product-led loop depends on it and it is not visible in current data. Test: in a closed beta, measure invite-to-payment within 72 hours and A/B pay-first vs account-first invitee flows; decide the unpaid-share policy before beta.

## V1 GTM Strategy One-Pager
- **Positioning:** For trip organizers who end up chasing friends for replies and money, Roamly Groups is a group-booking feature for curated local experiences that lets one person book, split and plan a whole party from a single link. Unlike a group chat, a payments app and a spreadsheet stitched together, it collects each person's share up front and keeps everyone on one itinerary, so the organizer never fronts the cost.
- **Key messages:** (1) One link gets everyone in and paid up. (2) You never front the money or chase anyone. (3) Everyone sees the plan and their own share before they pay.
- **Channel approach:** Owned first: in-app prompts triggered by group-booking behavior, email to users with abandoned group sessions, and the invite link itself as the growth channel. Earned: organizer and invitee sharing, plus the reviews and travel communities where Roamly's reputation was built. Paid: none at launch; held until the loop and cost per activated account are proven.
- **Success metric:** North-star: fully paid group bookings per month. Leading: at least 70% of invited participants pay within 72 hours (proposed); at least 2 new-to-Roamly accounts per group (proposed). Guardrail: individual-booking conversion within +/-2% of holdout. All targets are proposals, re-set against the week-0 baseline.
