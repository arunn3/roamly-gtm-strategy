# Roamly Groups · Pricing & Packaging Recommendation, Module 5

## Pricing Goal
- **Goal:** Enter a new market (penetration / share)
- **Why:** The M2 bet depends on a product-led loop: organizers bring in invitees and invitees create new accounts. Any friction in price works against that loop, especially on the invitee side, where trust in a platform they do not know is the riskiest assumption. Groups is a new behavior for Roamly, so adoption has to come before margin optimization. Revenue still arrives from recovered group sessions that used to drop off, larger baskets (several tickets per booking) and existing host commission.

## Pricing Model
- **Model:** Hybrid (combination)
- **Who pays & for what:** Transaction-based at the core (value is event-driven: each group booking), as a hybrid with a tiered ladder. The organizer pays nothing to organize, because the organizer is the loop starter and a subscription would charge a behavior that happens a few times a year. Invitees pay for their own share of the experience and are the most fee-sensitive party. Default hypothesis: any service fee (middle tier only) is folded into the per-person price shown up front (all-in), the way Viator and GetYourGuide show prices with no separate checkout fee, rather than added as a surcharge at checkout. Who bears the fee is the biggest open question. Cost check: splitting a $480 order into eight payments costs about $2.10 more to process (about 0.4% of the basket) at a 2.9% + $0.30 benchmark.

## Packaging Approach
- **Structure:** Combination
- **Audiences & fit:** Two audiences from M4 (organizer and invitee). One Good-Better-Best ladder serves both because the choice sits with the organizer; the invitee never chooses a tier and only sees their exact share.
- **Tiers:** (1) Group Basics - free to the organizer, no group service fee; up to ~6 people; invite link, each person pays their own share, shared itinerary. (2) Group Plus (marked as the Goldilocks middle) - up to ~15 people; small all-in per-person service fee, hypothesis range 0-5% of each share (test cells 0%, 3%, 5%); everything in Basics plus payment deadlines and automatic reminders, availability polls and preference voting (subject to V1 scope), spots held until the payment deadline (subject to host and Finance rules), multi-experience itineraries. (3) Group Concierge - 16+ people; waitlist / custom quote at launch with no new product built; host coordination for private-group requests, invoicing only if demand is proven; it exists to measure large-group and corporate demand without breaking the no-enterprise-features-at-launch commitment. All prices are hypotheses: no willingness-to-pay data and no commission data were provided.

## Research to Validate
- **Method:** Van Westendorp
- **Assumption to validate:** Organizers and invitees will accept a small, all-in per-person fee on Plus (0-5%) in exchange for removing fronting and chasing, without lowering the share of invitees who pay. Commission Van Westendorp for the acceptable per-person fee range and Max-Diff to rank Plus features (reminders, polls, spot hold, multi-experience itineraries), recruited against the ICP: organizers who planned a group experience in the last 12 months (users and non-users) plus a separate invitee sample, sliced by age and group size. Confirm in the closed beta with fee cells (0%, 3%, 5%) compared on the invitee payment rate.
- **Biggest risk if wrong:** Who bears the fee. If invitees punish any visible group fee, the whole loop slows; the right answer then is to absorb the fee in host commission (or charge nothing) and monetize through volume instead.

## One-Page Recommendation
We recommend a transaction-based hybrid with three Good-Better-Best group tiers (free Basics, a Plus tier with a small all-in per-person fee, and a Concierge waitlist) for Roamly Groups, to prove group booking is a repeatable behavior and seed the invite loop, because the organizer's burden is the unsolved problem and the one thing that could break the loop is friction on the invitee side. FINANCE: splitting a payment costs only about 0.4% of the basket more to process at benchmark rates, so the economics hold; revenue comes from recovered drop-off and bigger baskets, with the Plus fee as upside, and Finance owns the rollback threshold. SALES LEADERSHIP (Host Partnerships, who close deals on the supply side): hosts get larger confirmed parties, payments secured before the day and fewer no-shows (to verify with hosts); the Concierge waitlist logs bigger and corporate requests without a new product. PRODUCT MARKETING: 'one link, everyone pays their own share' is the story, so the price must never contradict it; an all-in per-person price keeps the invitee's number simple and free Basics proves 'no fronting'. VALIDATE FIRST: the invitee payment rate under three fee cells (0%, 3%, 5%) in the closed beta, before any fee is turned on at launch, paired with Van Westendorp for the range and Max-Diff for tier contents.
