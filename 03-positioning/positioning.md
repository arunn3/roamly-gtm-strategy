## Persona (who we're positioning for)

*An illustrative composite, not a real person.*

- **Who are they?** The friend who always ends up planning. Say a 20s–30s traveler who has booked on Roamly before, now organizing a long weekend with seven friends for a birthday. They're the one with the spreadsheet and the Venmo requests. (Group money friction is highest among Gen Z and Millennials *[S1]*; exact age skew is A6.)
- **What are they trying to accomplish?** A great shared experience that actually happens, with everyone in and paid, without losing weeks to logistics or getting stuck with the bill.
- **What's getting in their way?** Chasing replies, fronting money, tracking who owes what, and the awkwardness of asking friends to pay. 45% of group travelers report financial conflict or discomfort *[S1]*.

## 1. Positioning framework (six components)

| Component | Your answer |
|---|---|
| **FOR… Target customer** — who is it for? | The friend who ends up organizing everyone's group trip (5–12 people), already a Roamly user. |
| **WHO WANT/NEED… The need** | To get a whole group booked, paid and on the same plan without becoming the unpaid travel agent, fronting the money or chasing replies. |
| **WE OFFER… Market category** — what is it? | **Group booking for curated local experiences.** *Category play: reframe.* We accept the "book local experiences" category but change what matters in it: from catalog size and price to how much work the organizer carries. |
| **BECAUSE OF… Key benefit** — the #1 value | One link gets everyone in and paid: each person pays their own share, and the organizer never fronts the cost. |
| **DESPITE… Barriers** | Roamly Groups is brand new, so it has no group-specific track record yet. Invitees have to trust a platform they may not know and may need an account. V1 covers Roamly's own experiences, not whole trips (no flights or lodging), and isn't built for corporate or very large groups. |
| **BETTER THAN… Competitive alternative + differentiator** | Better than the DIY stack (group chat, Venmo/Splitwise, spreadsheet), which tracks money after the fact and leaves booking and fronting to the organizer, and better than tour marketplaces, which sell fixed group tours without a described split-payment flow *[S2][S3][S4]*. **Differentiator:** booking, payment and plan in one flow, on vetted local experiences. |
| **Proof** — evidence it's true | Roamly: 12,000+ vetted hosts, 40 cities, repeat booking at 3x the industry average *[Brief]*. Demand: users are already forcing group bookings through the product *[Brief]*. Market: 45% of group travelers have experienced financial conflict or discomfort, 82% would overpay to avoid it *[S1]*. Product proof is **pending the closed beta**: the share of invitees who pay within 72 hours and the number of groups fully paid. |

## 2. Core product truth

**Roamly Groups is the one place where a group's booking, payment and plan live together, so the organizer's job disappears.**

Among the alternatives reviewed, none combines a booking marketplace, split payment and a shared itinerary: planning apps don't collect payment, payment tools have no shared itinerary editing, and tour marketplaces sell fixed group tours *[S2][S3][S4][S5]*. This is a claim to re-verify hands-on before launch (A9). It also holds for both audiences: the organizer sees relief, and the invitee sees a clear plan and their exact share.

## 3. Positioning statement

> For **the friend who ends up organizing everyone's group trip** who **wants the whole group booked and paid without fronting the money or chasing anyone**, **Roamly Groups** is the **group booking tool for curated local experiences** that **gets everyone in and paid with a single link**. Unlike **a group chat, a payments app and a spreadsheet stitched together**, Roamly Groups **handles the booking, each person's payment and the shared plan in one place, with vetted local hosts**.

**One-liner (for the deck):** *Book the whole group in one link. Everyone pays their own share.*

*Note on the mistakes to avoid (Module 3):* the statement positions *for* the organizer, not against a competitor; it describes the combination of capabilities rather than a single feature; and the one-liner is kept separate from the statement, because a tagline doesn't do a statement's job.

## 4. Pressure tests

*Desk assessment of the draft statement against each test (not yet run with real people). The real five-customer test happens with organizers in the closed beta.*

| Test | Pass? | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Ownable** — could a competitor say the same? | **Partial** | "One link, everyone pays their share" is the most copyable phrase; Viator or Airbnb could adopt it. What's ownable is the *combination*: group flow plus vetted local hosts plus a repeat-booking record *[Brief]*. Fix: lead messaging with the organizer-relief outcome and Roamly's curation, not the payment mechanic alone. |
| **Believable** — is the proof real? | **Conditional** | Market proof is sourced *[S1]*, and brand proof comes from the brief. Product proof for Groups itself doesn't exist until the beta. Fix: do not publish performance claims until the beta numbers are in. |
| **Relevant** — does the ICP care? | **Pass** | The pain (fronting, chasing, awkward money asks) matches what organizers already do in Roamly's data *[Brief]* and what the market reports *[S1]*. Validate with five real organizers. |
| **Durable** — holds as the product grows? | **Pass, with a watch item** | The organizer-relief truth extends to families and wedding parties. If Roamly later adds corporate features, check against positioning drift: does the new thing serve the original audience, or widen it by default? |

## 5. Positioning vs. messaging note

Positioning stays fixed. Messaging changes by surface and audience:

- **Homepage headline (marketing):** "Group trips, without the group chat chaos." *Leads with the outcome in the customer's words.*
- **Sales opener** (Host Partnerships, to hosts): "Bigger parties, confirmed and paid up before they arrive." *Same truth, told for the supply side.*
- **App store / in-app blurb:** "Plan it once. Share one link. Everyone pays their own share." *Same promise, shortest form.*

A message that drifts from the position (for example, one that sells only "cheaper" or "more options") gets flagged against the statement above.
