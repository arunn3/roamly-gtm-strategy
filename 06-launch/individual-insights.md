## 1. Launch plan

**Launch type:** closed beta first, then a coordinated launch to existing users. **Tier 2** (focused GTM plan), matching Deliverable 1. **Release vs. launch:** the product is ready *[Brief]*, but the *launch* waits for beta evidence. We release to a small group, learn, and then launch.

| Phase | Goal | Key activities | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-launch** (W-8 to W-5) | Align the company and remove the biggest unknowns before any customer sees it. | Leadership signs off on the strategy, metrics and tripwires · pull the week-0 baseline for group-session drop-off (A2) · decide the unpaid-share policy (A5) · commission the Van Westendorp and Max-Diff studies (Deliverable 5) · instrument analytics · write enablement briefs · lock positioning before any asset is built | Product (lead), Analytics, Finance, Legal / Trust & Safety, PMM |
| **Closed beta** (W-4 to W-1) | Validate the riskiest assumption: invitees join and pay fast (A3). | Invite a small group of high-value organizers (users who start group-sized bookings or were flagged by Support) · owned channels only · test two invitee flows (pay-first vs. account-first) and the fee cells (0%, 3%, 5%) · collect real numbers and named testimonials to fill the Evidence layer · fix top friction | Product, Support, CS, Analytics |
| **Launch** (W0, a Tue–Thu, away from holidays and competitor announcements) | One coordinated moment to existing users, with every team telling the same story. | In-app prompt for organizers starting group bookings · launch email to users with group-session history · landing page and homepage headline · host announcement · 48-hour support watch · light earned push to the travel communities and review platforms where Roamly's reputation was built *[Brief]* | Marketing, Product, Support, Host Partnerships |
| **Post-launch** (W+1 to W+12) | Compound the momentum and learn what to do next. | Lifecycle prompt after a trip ("plan the next one") · case studies from beta organizers · review fee results and set the Plus price · 30/60/90-day reviews · decide whether to test paid acquisition (only if the loop and unit economics hold) · decide the next segment (families, wedding parties) from data | Product, Marketing, Finance, CS |

**Channels (owned → paid → earned):** Start owned: in-app prompts, email and the invite link itself. Earned channels come from the loop: organizers and invitees sharing, plus reviews. Paid is held until the loop and cost per activated account are known.

**Enablement order for a product-led launch (Module 6):** Leadership → Support → Customer Success → Host Partnerships and sales → Marketing last, because positioning must be locked first. Support comes before Sales because self-serve users arrive first.

| Team | What they need (the brief) | Ready when… |
|---|---|---|
| **Leadership** | Strategy, metrics, tripwires, 30-day monitoring plan | Metrics are agreed and a weekly review is scheduled |
| **Support** | FAQ, known limitations, escalation criteria (unpaid shares, refunds, host cancellations) | They can handle the first 48 hours without the PM |
| **Customer Success** | What was promised, the three tiers, the organizer use cases | They can explain Basics / Plus / Concierge without the deck |
| **Host Partnerships** | How group bookings and deposits work, party-size handling, no-show protection (A7), the host announcement | A sample of hosts has confirmed capacity and questions are answered |
| **Marketing** | Locked positioning, pillars, asset review against the framework | Every asset is checked against the positioning statement |

## 2. Launch metrics & guardrails

*All thresholds are **proposed** and get re-set against the week-0 baseline (A2).*

- **Success looks like:** At least 70% of invited participants pay within 72 hours · at least two new-to-Roamly accounts per group · group-session completion closes at least half the gap to individual-session completion · individual-booking conversion stays within ±2% of the holdout · organizers create a second group within the following 60 days (repeat behavior).
- **Every metric points to a decision (Module 6):**

| Metric | What it tells us |
|---|---|
| Acquisition by channel (organizers started, invitees opened) | Whether the owned channels reach the right people |
| Activation (group fully paid; invitee paid within 72 hours) | Whether messaging and onboarding delivered |
| Conversion on the Plus tier and fee cells | Whether pricing and packaging resonated |
| Competitive displacement (organizers who drop the DIY stack; repeat groups) | Whether positioning held up |
| Support tickets per group and host complaints | Whether the enablement was complete |

- **Tripwire (when to pull back):**
  - Individual-booking conversion falls more than 3% against the holdout for two consecutive weeks → pause new exposure and investigate.
  - Fewer than 40% of invitees pay within 72 hours by week 3 of the beta → stop the rollout and fix the invitee flow.
  - Refund, chargeback or host-cancellation rates exceed twice the individual-booking baseline → pause fees and review policy.
  - Any fee cell lowers the invitee payment rate against the 0% cell → remove that fee.

## 3. The full GTM story in one slide

Roamly has a loved product, proven repeat behavior and a clear signal: users are forcing **group bookings** through a product that wasn't built for them *[Brief]*. Because the pain is the **organizer's burden** (effort, fronting the money, chasing friends), not a booking gap, the right first customer is the **friend-group organizer who is already a Roamly user**. That customer buys and uses the product themselves in minutes, so the motion is **product-led**, and each organizer brings in 4–11 invitees, which makes the invite link the growth channel (Deliverable 1).

The competitor that matters most is the **DIY stack** of group chat, payments app and spreadsheet. Every tool in the market solves only a slice of the job (Deliverable 2), so the **bet** is to make the organizer's job disappear. **Positioning** turns that into one promise (book the whole group in one link; everyone pays their own share) (Deliverable 3). **Messaging** speaks to two people, the organizer who wants relief and the invitee who wants clarity (Deliverable 4). **Pricing** keeps the loop frictionless: free to organize, a small all-in fee on the middle tier, and a waitlist to size larger and corporate demand (Deliverable 5). The **launch** begins with a closed beta that tests the riskiest assumption first, then goes wide with the guardrails already agreed (this deliverable).

## 4. Individual insights

> **DRAFT for you to edit.** This section is meant to be your own honest reflection. The text below is written from what the work revealed and the judgment calls it forced; replace anything that doesn't match your experience.

- **Biggest "aha" across the certification:** The biggest competitor wasn't a company. It was the group chat, Venmo and spreadsheet that people already use and don't pay for. Once I framed it that way, the right product question changed from "how do we book for many people?" to "how do we make one person's burden disappear?". That reframing changed the segment, the bet and the pricing.
- **What changed in how I think about GTM:** I used to treat positioning, messaging and pricing as separate steps. Here they were one argument: the organizer's burden shaped the segment, which shaped the motion, which shaped what I could charge and who I could charge it to. A pricing choice that puts friction on invitees would have undone the product-led loop that the motion depends on. Also, the product-led loop is a design choice, not something that "just happens".
- **Friction points and how I worked through them:** (1) There was no Roamly data beyond the brief, so I had to separate what is known, what is sourced from outside, and what is an assumption, and I kept an assumption register so nothing looked more certain than it was. (2) Choosing between friend-groups and corporate offsites: corporate had a higher contract value, but the product likely wasn't ready and it needed a sales-led motion. Naming that as a deliberate sacrifice made the decision easier to defend. (3) Some of the best-looking competitor facts came from vendor-written guides, so I flagged them as indicative and noted that hands-on testing is still needed.
- **What I'd do differently with more time / data:** Pull the real baseline for group-session drop-off, test the invitee flow with real organizers before locking the bet, try the group flow on Viator, GetYourGuide and Airbnb hands-on, and run the blind read with actual people, since a self-check isn't a cold read.
- **The one idea I'll apply at work on Monday:** Before any launch plan, write down the single assumption that would break the strategy and the cheapest way to test it, and name what we're deliberately not doing.
