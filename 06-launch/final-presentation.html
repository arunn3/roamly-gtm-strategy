03 · POSITIONING

One link. Everyone pays their own share.

For the friend who ends up organizing everyone’s group trip, Roamly Groups is the group booking tool for curated local experiences that gets everyone in and paid with a single link. Unlike a group chat, a payments app and a spreadsheet stitched together, it handles booking, payment and the shared plan in one place, with vetted local hosts.

05 · PRICING RECOMMENDATION

Free to organize, priced to protect the loop

Group Basics

Up to ~6 people

Free to the organizer; no group fee

Invite link, split payments, shared itinerary

Group Plus ★

Up to ~15 people

Small all-in per-person fee (test 0%, 3%, 5%)

Basics + deadlines and reminders, polls, spot hold, multi-experience plans

Group Concierge

16+ people

Waitlist / custom quote

Host coordination for private groups; sizes corporate demand, nothing built

Model: transaction-based hybrid. Goal: prove group booking is a repeatable behavior and seed the invite loop. Prices are hypotheses (no willingness-to-pay data yet).

Validate first: who bears the fee. Van Westendorp for the range, Max-Diff for tier contents; beta fee cells compared on the invitee payment rate.

Cost check: splitting $480 into eight payments costs ~$2.10 more to process (about 0.4% of the basket) at a 2.9% + $0.30 benchmark [S5].

Benchmarks: SquadTrip 6% traveler-paid fee; WeTravel 2.5% [S5, vendor-published]; Viator keeps ~20% of gross [S2].

06 · INDIVIDUAL INSIGHTS

What building this taught me

Biggest aha

The biggest competitor wasn’t a company. It was the group chat, Venmo and spreadsheet people already use. Reframing the job from “book for many” to “make one person’s burden disappear” changed the segment, the bet and the pricing.

What changed

Positioning, messaging and pricing are one argument, not three steps. A fee that adds friction for invitees would have broken the loop the motion depends on.

Friction and next time

No Roamly data beyond the brief, so every claim is sourced or labeled an assumption. Next: pull the real baseline, test the invitee flow with organizers, and run a true blind read.

Monday: write down the one assumption that would break the strategy, and the cheapest way to test it.

APPENDIX · METRICS AND GUARDRAILS

Success metrics and tripwires

SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

  • North-star: fully paid group bookings per month
  • 70%+ of invitees pay within 72 hours (proposed)
  • 2+ new-to-Roamly accounts per group (proposed)
  • Group-session completion closes half the gap to individual
  • Individual booking conversion within ±2% of holdout

TRIPWIRES: WHEN TO PULL BACK

  • Individual conversion down more than 3% for two weeks: pause exposure
  • Under 40% of invitees pay in 72 hours by beta week 3: stop and fix the flow
  • Refunds, chargebacks or host cancellations above 2x baseline: pause fees
  • A fee cell that lowers the invitee payment rate: remove it

All thresholds are proposals to re-set against the week-0 baseline.
